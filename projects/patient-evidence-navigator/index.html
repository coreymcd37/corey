Patient Evidence Navigator

Find trusted information, the strongest research, and trials for a health condition, then bring it to a doctor.

❓ How does this work? (tap to open)

Searching health topics on regular Google mixes real science with ads and false "miracle cures." This page sends your search only to places that check their facts: government health agencies, hospitals, medical journals, and trial registries.

  1. Type the condition (like "pancreatic cancer"). Add details such as the type, stage, or a treatment name if you know them.
  2. Add your city if you want trials and specialists near you.
  3. Tap through the tabs and open any card. Each opens in a new tab.
  4. Save questions from the last tab and bring them, with anything you find, to the doctor.

Key idea: one study, a video, or a story is not proof. The "Ask & stay safe" tab shows how to tell strong evidence from weak.

Information, not medical advice. Never stop or delay treatment because of something you read online. Show what you find to the care team first.

Questions to ask the doctor

    How strong is the evidence? (best to weakest)

    1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses: many studies combined.
    2. Randomized controlled trials: a fair head-to-head test in people.
    3. Large observational studies: watching what happens to many people.
    4. Small studies and case reports: hints only.
    5. Lab-dish or animal studies: a result in cells or mice is not a cure for people.
    6. Testimonials and social media: stories, not evidence.

    Red flags for fake cures

    • Promises a cure or "100% success."
    • Says doctors or drug companies are "hiding" it.
    • Relies on testimonials with no published trial.
    • Tells you to stop or skip standard treatment.
    • Expensive, sold by one seller, or pushes you to buy fast.
    • Says "natural" means safe. Supplements can interfere with cancer drugs, so always tell the care team.

    One-page brief for the care team

    Uses the condition, details and city typed at the top. Fill in what you know below. It builds a clean page you can copy, print, or save as a PDF to hand to the doctor or nurse, with your questions and the searches you used.

    Pull trials and studies into spreadsheets

    Uses the condition, details and city typed at the top. It saves trials.csv (studies enrolling now) and studies.csv (newest systematic reviews, meta-analyses and randomized trials). CSV files open in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

    ▶ Where and how do I run this? (tap to open)

    A script is a list of instructions for a computer. The easiest free place to run one, even on a phone, is Google Colab. It runs in your browser and needs no installing.

    1. Fill in the condition (and city) at the top of this page.
    2. Tap Copy script below.
    3. Tap Open Google Colab and sign in with a Google account. On iPhone, if it looks cramped, use Safari's "Request Desktop Website".
    4. Tap in the empty box, paste, then tap the ▶ play button.
    5. Wait a minute or two. A file called results.zip downloads by itself. Open it to find trials.csv and studies.csv. If nothing downloads, tap the folder icon on the left in Colab.
    6. Open the CSV in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets. Every row has a link to read more.

    On a computer: install Python from python.org, run pip install requests, save the script as search.py, then run python search.py. A results folder appears.

    Trial lists change often, so run it again whenever you want fresh results. Always confirm eligibility with the care team before contacting a trial.
